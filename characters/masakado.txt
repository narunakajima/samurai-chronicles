Taira no Masakado: powerfully built male warrior in his mid 30s, mid-Heian period (10th century, 930s Japan) — a Bando frontier horseman, NOT a Sengoku-era samurai.
Broad weathered face, heavy dark brows, short black moustache and trimmed beard, hard direct gaze. Sun-darkened skin of a man who lives outdoors on horseback.
Wears early o-yoroi: a boxy lamellar cuirass of small scales laced with dark blue silk cords, worn over a russet brocade robe with wide sleeves, large square shoulder guards (o-sode), and a low-domed iron helmet with wide flaring side plates (fukigaeshi). Hair gathered up under a black lacquered tate-eboshi cap when bare-headed.
Armed as a mounted archer first: a tall asymmetric lacquered longbow and a quiver of arrows at the right hip. His sword is a slender, only slightly curved kenukigata-tachi slung edge-down from the belt — never a Sengoku-period katana thrust through the sash.
No family crests, no printed banners, no European or later-period armour elements. Buildings around him are timber-and-thatch halls behind earth banks and ditches, or tile-roofed provincial government compounds — never stone castles with white keeps.
Bearing of a plainspoken provincial fighter rather than a courtier: physical, blunt, unshakably confident, more comfortable in a saddle than a hall.
