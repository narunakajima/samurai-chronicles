Ashikaga Yoshimitsu: the third Ashikaga shogun (born 1358, died 1408). Likeness decided in STEP 5-0 (2026-11-23) from the early-15th-century hanging-scroll portrait at Rokuon-ji (Kinkaku-ji), the image most viewers see when they look him up: a shaven-headed lay monk with a heavy, fleshy face and a short grey stubble beard.
FACE AND BUILD (from the Rokuon-ji portrait; keep in every scene where his face shows): a stout, heavy-set, broad-shouldered Japanese man with a large, high, rounded egg-shaped head; a broad, full lower face with fleshy cheeks and heavy jowls; heavy-lidded, slightly drooping eyes whose outer corners turn down, giving a calm, half-lidded, appraising gaze; thin, faint eyebrows set high above the eyes; a long, straight, narrow nose; a small mouth with full lips; large ears with long lobes. Composed and unhurried, seldom smiling; the bearing of a man used to being obeyed.
AGE: about twenty in 1378, mid-thirties in 1392-1394, thirty-six to thirty-seven when he takes Buddhist vows in 1395, mid-forties in 1401-1403, about fifty in 1408. He dies in 1408.
BEFORE the vows of 1395 (every scene set in 1394 or earlier): a lay nobleman-shogun in court style. His full head of hair is gathered into a small topknot hidden under a tall black lacquered court eboshi cap (tate-eboshi); forehead and crown are NOT shaved (no sakayaki; shaved crowns were not yet usual). Facial hair: at about twenty, only a faint thin moustache; in his thirties, a thin moustache and a small short dark beard on the chin. Everyday formal wear: a dark court robe (noshi or kariginu) in deep indigo, black or grey, with subtle tone-on-tone woven roundel patterns (no clan crest on court robes). At the highest ceremonies (as Chancellor, late 1394): full sokutai court dress, a black outer court robe (ho) of the highest rank over layered robes, a black lacquered kanmuri court cap with a stiff tail rising at the back, and a flat wooden shaku baton held in both hands. No armour, no sword in peaceful scenes.
FROM 1395 ONWARD (every scene set in 1395 or later): a Buddhist lay monk (nyudo). His head is completely shaved, smooth, with no topknot, no hair and no cap of any kind. Facial hair: a short, sparse stubble beard along the chin and jawline (dark with grey in 1395-1403, grey-white by 1408), no long beard. He wears a monk's robe of deep tea-brown silk; at formal moments a kesa of gold-patterned brocade draped over his left shoulder (as in the Rokuon-ji portrait), otherwise a plain dark kesa. He often holds a string of dark prayer beads (juzu) and a folded fan. He keeps the court rank of Junsango after the vows but holds no office; a quiet air of command remains in how others kneel around him. Do not mix the two looks within one scene; the change of 1395 is deliberate and historical.
Nothing European in architecture, dress or objects. No modern paper, no spectacles. Historical setting: Kyoto and the Kitayama villa, 1358-1408 (late Nanboku-cho / early Muromachi). Men in the street wear eboshi caps or plain tied hair; no shaved foreheads.
