Kondo Isami: solidly built, thickset male swordsman in his late 20s to mid 30s,
of average height but heavy through the shoulders, chest and neck — a farmer's
build, not a courtier's. Broad squarish face, heavy jaw, wide flat cheeks, thick
straight eyebrows, calm steady eyes set well apart, and a notably large wide
mouth. Clean-shaven, sun-touched rather than pale skin. Deliberately NOT the
fine-featured handsome type: plain, blunt, physically imposing, with an open
almost genial expression that turns immovably hard under pressure — the face of
a man everyone finds it natural to follow. Hair: a sohatsu (総髪) hairstyle — a
FULL, UNSHAVED head of hair combed straight back from the forehead and crown
with NO shaved pate (NOT the standard chonmage/sakayaki), gathered into a
simple queue or knot at the back of the head. This matches his surviving
Keio-era (1865-68) studio photograph by Uchida Kyuichi and the same sohatsu
style worn by contemporaries like Sakamoto Ryoma and Tokugawa Yoshinobu. Do
NOT depict a shaved forehead/crown at any point in the story — unlike many
common-soldier extras, Kondo himself never shaves his pate, in Kyoto or later.
Kyoto years (1863-1867): pale asagi light-blue haori with a white
mountain-stripe (dandara) pattern at the hem worn over a dark kimono and hakama,
katana and wakizashi at the hip. Later years (1867-1868): same sohatsu
hairstyle, uncut — he NEVER adopted Western military dress, unlike Hijikata
Toshizo. Formal dark brown or black kimono, haori and hakama, or a plain dark
sleeveless jinbaori overcoat over them, swords at the hip; matches the
surviving studio photograph, seated square and still. From late December 1867
his right shoulder is wounded — the right arm hangs stiffly and is never shown
raising a sword after that point. Grounded, weight-forward bearing; the
solidity of a man holding a line for other people.
