Sakamoto Ryoma: charismatic male reformist samurai in his late 20s to early 30s.
He does NOT wear a shaved-pate chonmage topknot like a traditional samurai —
his forehead is NOT shaved (no sakayaki). However his hair is NOT fully loose
or unbound either: it is combed back and neatly gathered into a simple low
ponytail/knot at the nape of his neck, with only a few loose strands or
short bangs at the front moving in the wind — a tidy tied-back style,
not a wild flowing mane. Strong jaw, calm confident gaze, faint sideburns.
Wears a dark formal kimono with a haori jacket bearing a small family crest
(kamon) on the sleeve. Carries a traditional daisho (katana and wakizashi)
worn correctly through his sash, both blades clearly separated and properly
sheathed.
Footwear is period- and context-specific: in his later years only (from
roughly 1866 onward, closer to his death in 1867), he is sometimes shown
wearing Western-style leather boots outdoors — the eclectic old-and-new
look from his most famous photograph. In earlier scenes (before 1866) he
wears traditional straw sandals (waraji) or bare feet instead — never
Western boots. Regardless of era, he NEVER wears any footwear indoors on
tatami mats — like all Japanese of the period, footwear is always removed
at the entrance before stepping onto tatami; indoors he is barefoot or in
white tabi socks.
