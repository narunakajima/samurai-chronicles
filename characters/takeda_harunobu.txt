Takeda Harunobu (the young Takeda Shingen, before he took Buddhist vows in 1559): a Japanese heir of the Takeda
house of Kai, nineteen years old in 1541 (born December 1521). Do NOT draw the later tonsured, bearded Shingen
(that look belongs to his 40s and later, after 1559, and must never appear here).
Young man of sturdy, broad-shouldered build, a firm square jaw, clean-shaven or at most a very faint moustache,
heavy straight brows and watchful, steady dark eyes: serious, composed and guarded beyond his years, never boastful.
Hair in the standard Sengoku fashion for a young samurai: shaved pate (sakayaki) with a small topknot (chonmage).
Indoors wears a plain dark kosode and hakama with a dark haori jacket; the Takeda diamond crest (Takeda bishi) is
small and subtle on the jacket. No crimson fur cloak, no gunbai, no elaborate armour in close-ups (those belong to
his later years). Not yet a famous general: an heir at a decisive moment.
His exact portraits at this age do not exist; keep invented details to a minimum, no scars or birthmarks.
