Ashikaga Yoshimasa: slender, average-height Japanese nobleman-shogun, the eighth Ashikaga shogun. Thirteen years old in the earliest scene (his formal appointment as shogun, 1449); late 20s during the succession crisis (1464-1465); early-to-mid 30s during the Onin War and his retirement as shogun (1467-1477); mid-40s to mid-50s in the later Higashiyama scenes (1482-1490). He takes Buddhist vows in 1485, and dies in 1490 aged 54.
Oval face, delicate and refined features, dark eyes that read as distracted, melancholy, or quietly absorbed rather than commanding — a man more comfortable examining a tea bowl than a battle map.
Scenes before 1485 (his time as shogun, the succession dispute, the Onin War, and the first years of building the Higashiyama villa from 1482): standard Muromachi-period sakayaki (shaved forehead) with a simple chonmage topknot, no beard. Scenes from 1485 onward, after he takes Buddhist vows as a lay monk (nyudo) at his Higashiyama villa: his head is fully shaved, no topknot and no chonmage of any kind — this is a deliberate, historically grounded change, not an inconsistency. Note the tonsure year is 1485, three years after construction of the villa began.
Before 1485: wears fine Muromachi-period hitatare or kosode robes in muted, elegant colors (deep indigo, grey, soft moss green), occasionally marked with the Ashikaga clan crest — two black horizontal bars inside a circle (maru ni futatsu-biki). From 1485 onward: wears the plain grey or undyed brown robes of a Buddhist lay monk instead of court dress.
Rarely armed; carries no sword or weapon of any kind in almost every scene — at most a short ceremonial blade at his hip in the most formal court scenes. His hands are far more often occupied with a tea bowl, a folding fan, a calligraphy brush, an incense container, or a garden stone. Posture and bearing are withdrawn, aesthetic, and often visibly detached — even in scenes depicting political crisis or war, he should read as a man mentally elsewhere, absorbed in beauty rather than command.
Nothing European in architecture, dress, or objects around him. No spectacles, no modern paper — only traditional scrolls and washi. Do not confuse the pre-1485 sakayaki/chonmage with the post-1485 fully shaved monastic head; they belong to different periods of his life and must not be mixed within a single scene.
