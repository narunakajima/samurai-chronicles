Yamamoto Tsunetomo (山本常朝): lean, average-height Japanese man, depicted from his teens (as a page) through his sixties (as a monk).
Gaunt, intense face with deep-set eyes carrying a stern, unwavering severity — never boastful, never at ease in idle company.
Early life — page boy (age 9 until coming of age at 20): simple formal Edo-period boy's attire, child's hairstyle with an unshaved pate and long forelocks (maegami) — no shaved sakayaki, no swords worn.
Early life — adult retainer (from age 20 to 1700): simple dark Edo-period samurai attire, hair in a properly shaved sakayaki topknot, plain unadorned swords, understated formal court dress.
Later life (from 1700 onward, most of the story): shaved head as a Buddhist monk, plain grey-brown monastic robes (koromo) with no ornamentation, seated in solitary stillness inside a modest mountain hermitage.
Quiet, austere bearing — speaks of death and duty with total composure.
Known for dictating the Hagakure from a remote mountain hermitage rather than for any deed on a battlefield; never depicted in combat or wielding a drawn sword.
