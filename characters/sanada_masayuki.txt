Sanada Masayuki: lean, wiry Japanese lord of modest height, depicted mainly between age 38 (Ueda, 1585) and early 50s (Ueda, 1600), as an old man of 64 only in the final exile at Kudoyama (1611).
Narrow, shrewd face with narrow watchful eyes that seem to be calculating something behind a calm expression; thin moustache and a faint thin beard (greying at the temples by 1600). A mountain lord's weathered, sun-browned skin, no bulk, quick economical movements.
Historically accurate Sengoku-period hairstyle at all times: sakayaki (shaved forehead and pate) with the remaining black hair (greying in his 50s) oiled and tied into a small samurai topknot (chonmage) — never a full head of loose hair and never a modern cut. As a boy page at Takeda Shingen's court (around age 7): unshaved head with a forelock, hair tied back, no shaved pate.
Wears practical dark lacquered armor with dark cords, plain and unostentatious, with the Sanada six-coin crest (rokumonsen: two rows of three round coins) as a small emblem on the helmet front and banners. In council he wears a plain dark kimono and formal hakama with the same crest. Carries a closed iron war fan. Not tall, not flashy: a quiet, patient look, often with a slight, unreadable half-smile.
Never European-looking in armor, dress, or surroundings. Never depicted shouting or swaggering.

[Likeness evidence note — verified 2026-10-01 (ep111 fact-check)]
- No reliable lifetime likeness exists. The only portrait traditionally identified as Masayuki ("Sanada Masayuki gazo", Ueda City Museum, with copies in Matsushiro/Ueda) is described only as "said to be" his late-life image; the sitter looks far older than his age at death (64), and one research theory holds the original actually depicts his son Nobuyuki (who lived to 93). Do NOT treat that portrait as an accurate likeness.
- Therefore the face above (lean, narrow, shrewd, thin moustache) is a creative inference consistent with period convention and his reputation, not documented fact. No contemporary record of his height, build or facial features has been found.
- Documented facts usable for depiction: born 1547; age ~38 at the first Ueda battle (1585), ~53 at the second (1600); exiled to Mount Koya then Kudoyama (from late 1600); died at Kudoyama 1611 (age 64 by Western count, 65 by Japanese count). Posthumous/Buddhist name Ichio Kansetsu. Whether he took tonsure during exile is not confirmed in primary sources found, so exile scenes keep an aged sakayaki topknot (thin white hair) rather than a fully shaved monk's head.
- Sons for multi-person scenes: Nobuyuki (b.1566) and Nobushige/"Yukimura" (b.1567) were adults with sakayaki and topknots by 1585 (about 18-19) and in their early thirties in 1600 — never draw them as children after 1585.
