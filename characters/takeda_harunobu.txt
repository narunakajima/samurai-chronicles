Takeda Harunobu (the young Takeda Shingen, before he took Buddhist vows in 1559): a Japanese heir of the Takeda
house of Kai, nineteen years old in 1541 (born December 1521). Do NOT draw the later tonsured, bearded Shingen
(that look belongs to his 40s and later, after 1559, and must never appear here).
Young man of sturdy, thick-set, broad-shouldered build with a thick neck; a broad, full face with a firm square jaw,
clean-shaven (no moustache, no beard), thick heavy straight brows and watchful, steady dark eyes: serious, composed
and guarded beyond his years, never boastful. (The broad face, heavy brows and solid build are the features of the
famous later portraits of Shingen; keep them so the viewer can see the future Shingen in the young heir.)
Hair in the standard Sengoku fashion for a young samurai: shaved pate (sakayaki) with a small, neat topknot (chonmage).
Indoors wears a plain dark kosode and hakama with a dark haori jacket; the Takeda diamond crest (Takeda bishi, four
diamonds) is small and subtle on the jacket. No crimson fur cloak, no gunbai, no white-maned helmet, no elaborate
armour in close-ups (those belong to his later years). Not yet a famous general: an heir at a decisive moment.
His portraits at this age do not exist; keep invented details to a minimum, no scars or birthmarks.
