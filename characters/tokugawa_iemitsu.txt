Tokugawa Iemitsu: the third Tokugawa shogun (born 1604, shogun 1623-1651, died 1651 aged 46 by Western count). Nineteen at his accession in 1623, about thirty at the 1633-1635 edicts, mid-thirties at the 1639 ban on Portuguese ships, early forties in the mid-1640s, died in 1651.
Broad, firm, slightly rounded face with heavy-lidded, watchful eyes and a closed, unsmiling mouth. From his twenties on: a neat, trimmed moustache of moderate thickness above the lip, chin and cheeks clean-shaven, no full beard. At nineteen: a smooth, clean-shaven young face with no moustache. A heavy, still, commanding presence, taller and fuller in the shoulders than a courtier, with the stern calm of a man used to being obeyed rather than loved. Slightly heavier face and a tired look around the eyes in his forties. No visible scars or pockmarks.
Standard Edo-period sakayaki from his coming of age (genpuku, 1620) onward: forehead and crown shaved bare, remaining hair oiled and tied into a small neat chonmage topknot (never a full head of hair, never a modern haircut, never a shaved monk's head; he never took Buddhist tonsure). As a child before genpuku (the 1610s nursery scenes): uncut childhood hair with a forelock over the brow and the rest tied back, no shaved pate.
Wears dark ceremonial kamishimo or noshime with the Tokugawa three-leaf hollyhock (aoi) crest inside a circle on the shoulders and chest in state scenes; plain dark kosode and haori in private scenes. No armour in peacetime scenes. At most a closed folding fan.
