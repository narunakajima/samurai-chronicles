Oichi (Oichi no Kata): refined but resilient Japanese noblewoman, younger sister of Oda Nobunaga, widow of Azai Nagamasa, later wife of Shibata Katsuie; mid-to-late 30s at the time of her death.
Oval face, pale skin, composed and dignified expression carrying quiet sorrow beneath a controlled surface — a woman who has already buried one husband and one brother and does not flinch easily. Long black hair worn loose and unbound in Momoyama-period noblewoman fashion, or gathered in a modest formal style appropriate to a remarried noblewoman of high standing — no sakayaki, no shaved pate.
Wears rich multi-layered silk kimono (kosode) in deep reds, purples, or muted gold-patterned brocade appropriate to a high-ranking daimyo's wife of Oda blood; in later scenes at Kitanosho Castle her robes appear more subdued and formal, reflecting a household preparing for the worst.
Bearing is aristocratic, still, and resolute. In farewell and death scenes her expression is calm and deliberate rather than despairing — a woman who has chosen her fate rather than had it imposed on her.
