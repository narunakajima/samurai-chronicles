Okubo Toshimichi (1830-1878): a Satsuma samurai who became the Meiji government's most powerful statesman (finance minister, then first Home Minister), assassinated at Kioizaka in Tokyo on 14 May 1878 at age 47. Appearance changes with the year, so match every scene to its date.

Face and build (constant): a lean, long face with a high, broad forehead, deep-set sharp, piercing dark eyes under straight heavy eyebrows, a firmly closed mouth, a stern, composed, hard-to-read expression that almost never smiles. Tall and lean for a man of his time (a contemporary recalled about five shaku five sun, roughly 167 cm), thin as a boy. Upright, controlled bearing; his authority comes from stillness and restraint, not volume. Normal human proportions.

1830s to 1871 (Satsuma samurai and early Meiji official): sakayaki hairstyle (forehead and crown shaved, small chonmage topknot). Clean-shaven in his youth and thirties (no beard or heavy moustache), at most a faint, neatly trimmed moustache by 1871. Plain dark kimono, haori and hakama with daisho at the hip; formal dark robes for state occasions.
Age 19 (1850): youthful, thin and slight, clean-shaven, worn low-ranking clothing.

From the Iwakura Mission (Dec 1871) onward: the topknot is cut off (the mission's 1872 photographs already show him with short hair in Western dress). HIS HAIR IS HISTORICALLY DISTINCTIVE: NO topknot and NO shaved crown, short dark cropped zangiri-style hair combed and parted at the side, fully covering his scalp; deliberately NOT the sakayaki-and-chonmage look. 1872: moustache joined to side whiskers. From 1873 to 1878, as in his well-known surviving photographs and a contemporary's description: a thick jet-black moustache joined to full whiskers and a beard running from the cheeks down to the chin. Dark Western wool suit or frock coat with a high collar; Japanese clothes at home are possible but the hair stays short.

Note: his photographs are genuine photographs (unlike Saigo, who left none). Details of facial hair in the 1860s are not documented, so keep it modest and do not invent a beard before 1872.
