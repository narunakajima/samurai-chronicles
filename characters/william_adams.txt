William Adams (Miura Anjin): Photorealistic, live-action film still — not a painting, not digital
illustration, not anime. Weathered English navigator in his 40s, sun-worn face, sharp pale eyes.
Reddish-brown hair, usually swept back. A reddish-brown, short unkempt beard and mustache
consistently present in every single scene without exception — he is never clean-shaven, never
smooth-jawed; this is a fixed trait that must not drift, and stands in deliberate contrast to
clean-shaven Japanese characters such as Hasekura. Physically imposing, sailor's build, weathered
hands. In early scenes (shipwreck, imprisonment, before c.1604): worn, salt-stained European
sailor's clothing only — no armor, no swords, no samurai rank, consistent with his status as a
castaway with no official standing yet. In later scenes (after Ieyasu grants him samurai status,
c.1604 onward): a hybrid of European and Japanese dress — Japanese kimono/hakama layered with
European touches such as boots or a doublet, exactly two swords (daisho: one long katana, one
short wakizashi) worn sheathed at the belt in the samurai manner despite his foreign face and
reddish beard — never a third blade, never a duplicate or extra scabbard, never a sword drawn
or held in hand unless the scene explicitly calls for combat. Carries himself with the calm
authority of a man who has survived shipwreck, imprisonment, and reinvention.
