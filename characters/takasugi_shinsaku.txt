Takasugi Shinsaku: Photorealistic, live-action film still — not a painting, not digital
illustration, not anime. A slight, slender Japanese samurai of the Choshu domain, aged 17 to 27
(youthful and restless in early scenes, visibly thinner and more gaunt in the final scenes of his
illness). Individual features based on his two surviving studio photographs (taken by Ueno Hikoma
in Nagasaki, 1865 and 1866) and contemporary accounts: a noticeably LONG, NARROW face (a well-known
Japanese quip says even the horse he rode had a rounder face than he did), narrow sharp eyes,
strong thin eyebrows, faint smallpox pockmarks on his cheeks (he survived smallpox at about ten),
slight, not imposing build, and an intense, defiant gaze. Clean-shaven — no mustache, no beard, no
stubble — consistent in every scene.

HAIRSTYLE CHANGES DURING HIS LIFE — match each scene's date:
- Before spring 1863 (Hagi youth, Shanghai 1862, the legation burning of January 1863): standard
  samurai sakayaki shaved crown with a folded chonmage topknot.
- Spring to late 1863: he shaved his head in a Buddhist tonsure (taking the name Toko) — head
  close-shaven, only short dark stubble regrowing, no topknot.
- From 1864 to his death in 1867 (Kozan-ji uprising, Kiheitai, 1866 war, deathbed): NO topknot and
  NO shaved crown. Short dark cropped hair (zangiri) about ear length, combed and loosely parted to
  one side, fully covering his scalp, exactly as in his 1866 photograph. This was extremely unusual
  for a samurai of the time and is deliberately NOT the sakayaki-and-chonmage look of the other
  samurai around him. Every image_prompt for these scenes must restate this explicitly.
- At the September 1864 peace talks aboard the British flagship he wore a formal daimon robe of
  yellow cloth with large light-blue crests and a black silk eboshi court cap (per Ernest Satow).

Dark haori and hakama with a daisho (katana and wakizashi) worn correctly through the sash; in the
Kiheitai and 1866 war scenes he may wear a practical dark jacket over hakama, but never a Western
military uniform and never Western boots (straw sandals or tabi only). Carries himself with
reckless, theatrical confidence; in the last scenes he is pale and thin, coughing into a cloth,
yet his gaze is unchanged.

Evidence note: the face and 1866 hairstyle come from genuine photographs of Takasugi himself (not
posthumous imagined portraits). Accounts of his height conflict (some call him small and slight,
Miura Goro recalled him as tall and slim), so keep the build simply slender and wiry.
