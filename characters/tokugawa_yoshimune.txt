Tokugawa Yoshimune: tall, sturdy, vigorous Japanese lord, the eighth Tokugawa shogun (born 1684, shogun 1716-1745, died 1751). Early 30s when he becomes shogun (1716), late 30s at the 1722 edicts, 40s through the 1720s-30s, early 60s at his 1745 retirement, mid-60s at the end of his life.
Traditionally described as unusually tall (about six shaku, roughly 180 cm) and physically powerful, with a dark, sun-tanned complexion (iro-guro) from an outdoor, martial life of riding and falconry; show him clearly taller and more heavily built than the men around him, with weathered darker skin rather than pale courtly skin. (Note: the height comes from tradition; a mortuary-tablet theory suggests about 155 cm, so do not exaggerate him into a giant.)
Broad, strong-jawed face with a firm mouth and steady, watchful, practical eyes; an air of calm, no-nonsense authority rather than courtly refinement. Clean-shaven or at most a faint shadow, no beard. Dark hair through his 30s-40s, greying in his 50s, thin white-grey in his 60s.
Standard Edo-period sakayaki: forehead and crown shaved bare, remaining hair oiled and tied into a small neat chonmage topknot (never a full head of hair, never a modern haircut, never a shaved monk's head; he remains a topknotted layman after retiring in 1745).
Wears plain, undyed or dark cotton kosode and a simple haori in most scenes, deliberately unadorned; in formal state scenes dark ceremonial kamishimo or noshime with the Tokugawa three-leaf hollyhock (aoi) crest inside a circle on the shoulders and chest. Never armor in peacetime scenes. Carries at most a folding fan; in leisure scenes a falconer's glove with a hawk on his fist.
