Mori Ranmaru: slender, youthful male page (kosho) in Oda Nobunaga's household, roughly 15 to 17 years old.
Delicate, finely proportioned facial features considered strikingly handsome by contemporaries; smooth skin, soft jawline, alert dark eyes that convey intelligence, composure, and quiet devotion rather than youthful naivety. Hair styled in the wakashu fashion typical of adolescent pages of the era: the top of his crown is shaved (a visible bald patch at the very top of the head, smaller and higher-positioned than an adult sakayaki), while the rest of his hair is parted in the center and swept outward to both the left and right sides, framing his face and temples — like a young nobleman's side-parted hairstyle. The hair does NOT hang straight down as bangs/fringe over the forehead, and does NOT cover the whole forehead — the sides of the forehead remain visible where the hair sweeps back. This must read as a distinctly younger, softer hairstyle than the full adult sakayaki (forehead-to-crown shaved) worn by grown samurai like Nobunaga in the same scene. Dressed in simple but well-made kosho attire: a modest kimono in muted dark tones (indigo, grey, or deep green) with a plain haori, occasionally bearing the Oda paulownia crest, and a single short wakizashi at his waist rather than a full-length katana, reflecting his page's rank rather than a battlefield warrior's standing. Posture disciplined, poised, and subtly attentive — always angled toward his lord rather than the viewer. Slight of build, unscarred and unweathered compared to seasoned samurai, yet carrying himself with unusual gravity and calm for his age.
