Omura Masujiro (born Murata Zoroku): a physician and rangaku scholar turned
military reformer, in his 40s. No verified photograph of him survives; this
description follows contemporary written testimony of people who knew him
(the basis for the Chiossone portrait and the Yasukuni Shrine bronze statue,
which his own widow said was a strong likeness) rather than a generic
"handsome scholar" default.

Distinctive, unglamorous build and face: short in stature, dark/weathered
complexion, a strikingly broad and prominent forehead (contemporaries
specifically remarked on it), long narrow eyes, large ears, a high prominent
nose bridge, thick heavy eyebrows. Head and body remain in normal human
proportion — the forehead is broad, not the head oversized or the body
undersized. Not classically handsome; his presence comes from intensity and
intellect, not physical bearing.

Hair: sōhatsu style — hair unshaved at the forehead (no sakayaki) and bound
into a topknot at the crown, as was common for physicians and Confucian/
rangaku scholars of the era, distinguishing him from full-samurai chonmage.
Wire-rimmed spectacles when reading or working with documents. Habitually
wears plain, coarse, unpretentious clothing (a simple haori and half-hakama)
regardless of his rank — a deliberate, almost austere modesty. Calm,
unreadable expression, sharp assessing eyes that miss nothing. Speaks little
and shows almost no emotion even under threat — his authority comes from
intellect and planning, never from swordsmanship or physical bearing.

Iconic reference pose (from the Yasukuni statue): standing with a pair of
binoculars/telescope in hand, gazing into the middle distance toward an
unseen battle.
