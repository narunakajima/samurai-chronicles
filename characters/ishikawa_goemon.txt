Ishikawa Goemon: a big, powerfully built Japanese man in his late thirties (his exact age and origins are unknown), tall for his time, broad-shouldered and barrel-chested, with a thick neck, heavy forearms and large rough hands. Broad, strong weathered face with a heavy jaw, thick dark eyebrows, large intense dark eyes, and heavy blue-black stubble on the jaw and chin. Expression: bold, fearless and self-assured, a controlled half-smile of defiance that gives nothing away.
HAIR (the key identifying feature, different from every other man in the episode): his forehead and crown are NOT shaved — no sakayaki, and no chonmage topknot on the crown. He has a full, thick, coarse head of black hair grown out over the whole scalp, shaggy and untamed, about collar length, roughly swept back off the face and tied with a plain cord into a loose, untidy bundle at the back of the head (at the nape), with ragged strands falling over the forehead and temples. This is the realistic, toned-down version of the famous bushy "grown-out pate" hair of the Kabuki Goemon, not the giant stage wig.
Dressed like a rough Momoyama-era bandit: a worn dark charcoal or deep indigo cotton kosode, a plain thick padded dark outer coat over it, a rope or cloth sash with a single short sword, the hem tucked up, cloth leggings and straw sandals. In night scenes he wears the same dark clothes with a dark cloth tied over his nose and mouth only; his head is left uncovered so his shaggy tied-back hair stays visible (never a full ninja hood).
In the execution scenes (Kyoto, 1594) he wears a plain white prisoner's kosode, hands bound in front, the same face, build and unshaved hair, now more dishevelled with loose strands, head held high.
Not to include: Kabuki kumadori face paint, the huge stage wig, gold-brocade costume, and the long kiseru pipe (tobacco pipes were not yet in common use in Japan in 1594). No portrait of the real man survives; this look follows the popular image of Goemon from Edo-period theatre and prints, made realistic.
