Takeda Nobutora (1494-1574), head of the Takeda house of Kai and father of Harunobu (the later Shingen).
His face follows the posthumous portrait painted by his son Takeda Nobukado (Daisen-ji, Kofu, Important Cultural
Property): a lean, hard, weathered face with prominent cheekbones, a large, high-domed head with a broad forehead,
deep-set, glaring, piercing eyes that seem to pierce whoever he looks at, a thin, tight-lipped mouth, clean-shaven
(no beard, no moustache). Authority of a man who united his province by force; unyielding, never jovial.
Forty-seven in 1541 (layman): the same face, a little fuller and stronger, in his late forties; hair in the
standard Sengoku fashion, shaved pate (sakayaki) with a topknot (chonmage) streaked with grey. Wears a dark kosode
and hakama with a dark haori jacket; when travelling, on horseback with an escort in plain dark riding clothes.
No elaborate armour in close-ups.
He took Buddhist vows around 1543 (monastic name Mujinsai Doyu). From then on (his Kyoto years and old age) draw him
as a lay monk exactly as in the portrait: head shaved clean with NO topknot and NO hair, a white kosode under a
plain black (ink-dyed) monk's robe, a small patterned monk's stole (kara) hung from the neck, and sometimes a
rigid round fan held in one hand. Only 1541-early 1540s scenes keep the topknot.
In his old age (1570s, about eighty): thin and frail but still upright, shaven head, the same piercing eyes,
black monk's robe over a white kosode; keep it simple and dignified.
Do not invent scars, birthmarks, or other distinctive features. Do not draw him as a monster.
