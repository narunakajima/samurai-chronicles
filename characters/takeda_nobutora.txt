Takeda Nobutora (1494-1574), head of the Takeda house of Kai and father of Harunobu (the later Shingen).
Forty-seven in 1541: a stocky, hard, weathered man in his late forties with a stern, heavy-jawed face, a short
dark moustache with a little grey, and watchful, unyielding eyes. Authority of a man who united his province.
Hair in the standard Sengoku fashion: shaved pate (sakayaki) with a topknot (chonmage), touched with grey.
Wears a dark kosode and hakama with a dark haori jacket when travelling; on horseback with an escort in plain
dark riding clothes, no elaborate armour in close-ups.
He took Buddhist vows around 1543 (monastic name Mujinsai Doyu). From then on (his Kyoto years and old age) draw him
as a lay monk: head shaved clean with NO topknot, plain dark monk's robe. Only 1541-early 1540s scenes keep the topknot.
In his old age (1570s, about eighty): thin and frail but still upright, shaven head, plain dark monk's robe;
a posthumous portrait by his son Takeda Nobukado (Daisen-ji, Kofu) exists (details to be confirmed in STEP 5-0),
so keep it simple and dignified.
Do not invent scars, birthmarks, or other distinctive features. Do not draw him as a monster.
