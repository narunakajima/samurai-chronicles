Hojo Tokimune: Japanese regent (shikken) of the Kamakura shogunate in the late 13th century, born 1251, died 1284 aged 32 (34 by traditional Japanese count). Young, severe and self-contained. No authentic likeness survives: the Kamakura-period Zen-style seated portrait at Mangan-ji (Kumamoto), long labeled as Tokimune, is now generally identified as his cousin Hojo Sadamune, and the seated wooden statue at Butsunichi-an (Engaku-ji) is an Edo-period work. So never base him on those monk-style portraits; keep the look restrained and consistent.
Face: smooth oval face with strong straight brows, dark intense watchful eyes, a thin sparse moustache and a faint chin beard, lips pressed in grim control. As a teenager (1268): leaner, boyish but already unsmiling. In his twenties and early thirties: harder lines around the eyes, tired and shadowed, the bearing of a man who trusts no one.
Hair and headwear (Kamakura period, NOT Edo-style; applies to every scene before his death-day vows in 1284, including childhood after his coming-of-age ceremony in 1257): the forehead and crown are NOT shaved; black hair is combed back and gathered into a small topknot (motodori) and covered by a tall black lacquered eboshi cap tied under the chin. Never a shaved pate (sakayaki) or Edo-style chonmage.
Clothing: dark formal hitatare (wide-sleeved jacket and hakama) with a plain undyed collar, no visible crest or lettering; in military scenes an o-yoroi box-style armor with large square shoulder plates (osode), a horned kabuto, and a tachi sword at the hip. He commands from behind the lines and does not fight in melee.
After his vows on the day he died (April 1284): a shaved head and a plain dark Buddhist monk's robe with a kesa stole, an emaciated face, a prayer-bead rosary in his hands.
