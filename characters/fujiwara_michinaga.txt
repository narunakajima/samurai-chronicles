Fujiwara no Michinaga: Photorealistic, live-action film still — not a painting, not digital illustration, not anime. The most powerful Fujiwara nobleman of the Heian court, in his 30s to 50s (confident, broad-shouldered for a courtier, strong-featured face, dark hair with a few grey strands in later scenes, a short thin mustache, ambitious and sharp eyes, the easy self-assurance of a man who runs the government).
No reliable portrait survives, so keep him a generic, imposing Heian-period Japanese nobleman. Never a Western or modern face.
HAIR AND HEADWEAR (Heian period, NO shaved crown, NO chonmage): hair gathered into a topknot (motodori) under a stiff black lacquered-gauze kanmuri court cap with its upright tail at the back for formal court scenes, or a soft black eboshi cap in informal scenes.
CLOTHING: layered black sokutai court robe with a long trailing hem, stiff belt and a flat wooden ceremonial baton (shaku) for formal scenes; an informal dark noshi or kariginu robe over wide trousers at home. Soft silk, period-correct, no armor, no swords, no Edo-period clothing.
Carries himself with relaxed command — seated or standing, others defer to him. Props never carry writing.
Evidence note: his appearance is not documented; everything above is a period-appropriate generic depiction.
