Hojo Soun (Ise Moriji / Ise Shinkuro, later known by his Buddhist name Soun): a lean, wiry,
composed Japanese warlord of the late Muromachi / early Sengoku period. Alert, narrow,
watchful eyes and a calm, patient bearing: a planner who waits and calculates rather than a
loud, swaggering commander. Weathered face with age lines. His exact age is not settled (birth
year is debated), so depict him as a mature man in the middle scenes and plainly elderly, but
still upright and sharp-eyed, in the later scenes. Do not invent scars, birthmarks or other
distinctive features.
He took Buddhist vows (as "Soun'an Sozui") between 1491 and 1495, most likely at the time of
his 1493 move into Izu. Scenes up to about 1491 show him before vows; scenes from 1493 onward
show him as a tonsured lay monk.
Before taking Buddhist vows (Kyoto and Suruga, 1480s to about 1491): a Muromachi shogunate
official's look, full head of hair NOT shaved at the forehead or crown (no sakayaki, which was
not yet usual), gathered into a small topknot hidden under a soft black eboshi cap; a dark
hitatare robe, sober and unadorned (never an Edo-style kamishimo).
After taking Buddhist vows (from 1493): head shaved smooth with no topknot, wearing a simple dark
lay-monk's robe over a plain dark kimono; the same lean build and watchful eyes. He keeps a
warlord's quiet authority even in monk's dress.
Never a flamboyant general: no elaborate crest-covered armor in close-up scenes, no sword drawn
in anger in his own close-ups. Not related to the Kamakura-period Hojo regents (Masako,
Tokimune): this is the later "Go-Hojo" founder, who was an Ise clan man.
