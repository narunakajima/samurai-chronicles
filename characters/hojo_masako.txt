Hojo Masako: composed, strong-featured Japanese noblewoman of the Kamakura period (late 12th to early 13th century), wife of Minamoto no Yoritomo and later the de facto ruler of the Kamakura shogunate.
As a young woman (late teens to 20s): long straight black hair worn loose down her back in Heian court style, pale layered silk robes (kosode and uchiki), direct unflinching gaze — striking and determined rather than delicate.
As a mature woman (30s to 40s): darker, more restrained robes with minimal ornamentation, sharper and more guarded expression, the bearing of someone accustomed to being obeyed.
After taking Buddhist vows in 1199 (age 42 onward, the "nun shogun" years): dark grey or muted indigo Buddhist nun's robes over a plain white under-kimono, a simple dark kesa stole across one shoulder, hair cut short at shoulder length in the amasogi style — NOT a fully shaved head — often partly covered by a plain white cloth head covering.
Carries a Buddhist prayer-bead rosary (juzu) rather than any weapon. She NEVER wears armor, never carries a sword or naginata, and never appears on a battlefield — her power is entirely political.
In old age (60s): lined face, thinner frame, greying hair, but posture upright and eyes undimmed — authority expressed through stillness rather than gesture.
