Takenaka Hanbee (Takenaka Shigeharu): slight, sickly Sengoku-era strategist, appears mid-20s to
mid-30s across the story, growing visibly gaunt in later scenes.
Pale, thin face with sunken cheeks and calm, dark, unhurried eyes — the look of a man who reads a
room rather than dominates it. Slender, narrow-shouldered build, clearly less physically robust
than the warriors around him. In scenes depicting his final illness, may show a stooped posture,
hollowed features, or a suppressed cough.
Wears plain grey, brown, or muted indigo robes rather than ostentatious armor; light or no armor in
most scenes, reflecting his role as an advisor rather than a frontline combatant. When armor
appears at all, it is understated and worn loosely, never his defining feature.
Often seen holding maps, scrolls, or a plain war fan (gunbai) rather than a drawn sword.

HAIR — critical, frequently rendered wrong, read carefully: his hairstyle is exactly like a
Heian-period court noble (kuge) such as Fujiwara no Michinaga, NOT like the other samurai in this
series. Thick black hair covers his ENTIRE head with zero visible scalp anywhere — no shaved patch
on the crown, no shaved patch on the forehead, no bald spot, no visible skin on top of the head at
all, from the front hairline all the way back to the nape. The hair simply lies flat and smooth
over the whole skull, combed straight back, then gathered into one simple low ponytail-like knot
at the nape of the neck. Picture a modern man with an ordinary full head of straight black hair
combed straight back with no part — that is exactly what the top and front of his head should look
like, just tied back at the end. He does NOT have the shaved sakayaki pate or forward-folded
chonmage topknot that every other samurai character in this series has — this is what makes him
visually distinct and must never be defaulted back to the standard samurai look.

No elaborate ornamentation. Expression is composed, watchful, and contemplative — a strategist
whose authority comes entirely from his mind, never from physical presence.
