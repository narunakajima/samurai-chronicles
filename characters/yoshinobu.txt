Tokugawa Yoshinobu: a slender, fine-boned male Japanese aristocrat in his late twenties to early thirties, of average height and noticeably less heavy-set than the warrior lords around him — a scholar's frame rather than a swordsman's.
Long, narrow face with high cheekbones, a thin straight nose, and pale, indoor skin; sharp, half-lidded dark eyes that observe far more than they reveal.
Period-accurate sakayaki hairstyle at all times (this is the 1860s, before the 1871 haircut edict): forehead and crown of the scalp shaved bare, the remaining black hair oiled and gathered into a small, immaculately neat chonmage topknot folded flat against the crown — never a full head of loose hair, never a modern haircut, never Western-cropped hair.
Default attire is formal Japanese shogunal court dress — layered silk robes in deep black, indigo, or dark violet with subtle woven patterning, worn with hakama, plus stiff-shouldered kariginu or noshi-style ceremonial robes for state occasions. He is never dressed in Western military uniform or Western clothing of any kind, even though the troops of his army are French-trained and Western-uniformed; for travel or flight scenes he wears a plainer, darker unadorned kimono and hakama with a travel cloak, and during his period of seclusion a subdued, monastic-adjacent plain robe of undyed or grey cloth.
Bearing is upright, still, and precise, with the controlled economy of movement of a man who has been watched his whole life.
Expression is intelligent, guarded, and almost unreadable — courteous without warmth, calculating rather than emotive, a face that gives nothing away even under enormous pressure.
