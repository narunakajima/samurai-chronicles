Minamoto no Yoshiie: powerfully built male warrior, late Heian period (11th century Japan, active c. 1050s-1100s) — a mounted archer-general of the eastern frontier wars, NOT a Sengoku-era samurai.
Long angular face, thick dark brows, a hard commanding gaze, weathered sun-darkened skin of a man who has spent decades campaigning in the northern provinces. In his 20s to 40s across most scenes; in later scenes (1090s onward and old age) his hair and short beard show grey, his face lined with the strain of political disfavor.
Wears early o-yoroi: a boxy lamellar cuirass laced with dark indigo or deep red cords, worn over a plain brocade robe with wide sleeves, large square shoulder guards (o-sode), and a low iron helmet with wide flaring side plates (fukigaeshi), often with a crescent or horned frontal ornament (kuwagata). Hair gathered up under a black lacquered tate-eboshi cap when helmetless — no shaved-pate chonmage; that hairstyle is anachronistic to the 11th century and must not be used.
Armed above all as an archer: always carries a tall asymmetric lacquered longbow (yumi) and a quiver of arrows at the hip — his signature weapon and the source of his legend as "Hachiman Taro," eldest son of the war god. A slender, only slightly curved kenukigata-tachi sword slung edge-down from the belt, drawn only when the bow is spent — never a Sengoku-period katana thrust through the sash.
No family crests as later Sengoku clans used them, no printed banners, no stone castles. Backgrounds are timber palisade forts, snowbound northern stockades, or provincial government halls.
Bearing of an iron-willed frontier commander: stoic under hardship, fiercely loyal to his men, carrying himself with quiet authority that later curdles into weary defiance as the court turns against him.
