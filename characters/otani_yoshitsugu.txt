Otani Yoshitsugu: composed Japanese general of medium build, around 40 years old.
In his younger years: calm intelligent face, steady dark eyes, short trimmed beard, dark plain robes or practical field armor.
In his final years (from the late 1590s): gravely ill and blind. His head and face are wrapped in plain white cloth with only a narrow gap left, and he is seated upright inside an open black-lacquered palanquin (koshi) carried by retainers — never mounted, never standing.
Clothing depends on context: in domestic, court, or private indoor scenes (residence, veranda, tea room, private conversation before a campaign) he wears plain dark formal robes ONLY — no armor, no armor plates, no gauntlets visible under or over the robes. Armor (Sengoku-period tosei gusoku in muted dark blue-grey lacquered plates with white cord lacing) is worn only in military contexts — on campaign, in an army camp, marching with troops, or on a battlefield.
Bearing: absolute composure and stillness — a commander who listens instead of looking, and gives orders he cannot see carried out.
Depict the illness only through the white face covering and the palanquin. Never show facial disfigurement, lesions, or any sensational detail.
