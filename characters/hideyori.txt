Toyotomi Hideyori: young male lord in his early twenties, born into the highest luxury.
Contemporary and early Edo records (Meiryo Kohan; the Nagasawa Kikigaki account of the Osaka
campaigns, which calls him "of a corpulence without equal in the world") describe him as an
exceptionally large man — roughly 6 shaku 5 sun (about 197cm) and around 43 kan (about 161kg).
Depict him accordingly: unusually tall and heavily built, broad-shouldered and physically
imposing, visibly towering over the men around him.
Face follows the surviving Yogen-in portrait tradition: fair-skinned and youthful, with a full
face, his father Hideyoshi's broad forehead, and alert dark eyes — handsome rather than hardened.
Wears elaborate gold-and-black lacquered armor bearing the Toyotomi paulownia (kiri) crest in
military scenes; in formal court settings he wears a court noble's silk kariginu or noshi robe
with a tall black lacquered eboshi cap, reflecting his rank of Naidaijin (1603) and Udaijin (1605).
His posture is noble but uncertain — raised entirely within Osaka Castle's walls, he never had the
chance to become the warrior his lineage needed.
Carries the weight of Hideyoshi's impossible legacy in every expression: earnest, proud, and
ultimately overwhelmed by forces beyond his control.
