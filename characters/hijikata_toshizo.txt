Hijikata Toshizo: tall, strikingly handsome male swordsman in his late 20s to
mid 30s. Long oval face, high cheekbones, narrow sharp eyes, clean-shaven, pale
skin, a cold and controlled expression that rarely softens. Kyoto years
(1863-1867): traditional samurai topknot, pale asagi light-blue haori with a
white mountain-stripe (dandara) pattern at the hem worn over a dark kimono and
hakama, katana and wakizashi at the hip. Hakodate years (1868-1869): hair grown
out from the shaved topknot and combed straight back off the forehead to medium
length, worn loose and UNTIED — NOT a short Western military crop, and NOT tied
back in a ponytail, queue or bun of any kind. Matches the surviving 1869
Hakodate studio photograph exactly: hair simply combed straight back and left
hanging loose, resting near the collar, not cropped close to the scalp and not
gathered or bound at the back. Dark navy Western-cut military tunic with brass buttons,
matching trousers and riding boots, sword at the belt and a revolver holstered.
Precise, disciplined bearing; the stillness of a man who has already decided.
