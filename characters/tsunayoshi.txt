Tokugawa Tsunayoshi: the fifth Tokugawa shogun (born 1646, shogun 1680-1709, died 1709). Late thirties at the 1683 revision of the warrior code, late forties during his Confucian lectures of the early 1690s, about fifty in 1695, mid-fifties at the 1701 Ako incident.
Face (follows his best-known portrait, attributed to Tosa Mitsuoki, Tokugawa Art Museum, c.1685): a full, fleshy, pale face with heavy rounded cheeks and jaw and a soft double chin; a long, straight, high-bridged nose; thin high-arched eyebrows set well above narrow, heavy-lidded eyes that look slightly downward; a small mouth with full lips and faintly downturned corners. Clean-shaven, no beard, no moustache. A cool, aloof, scholarly, self-possessed expression rather than a warrior's toughness.
Build: not tall and not muscular; a soft, well-fed, compact frame under wide formal robes. Never a towering or athletic figure, but not a caricature either.
Hair: standard Edo-period sakayaki: forehead and crown shaved bare, remaining hair oiled and tied into a small neat chonmage topknot (never a full head of hair, never a modern haircut, never a shaved monk's head). Hair fully dark in his thirties and forties; a few grey strands at the temples by his mid-fifties.
Ageing: late thirties = smooth full face; about fifty = the same full face with faint lines at the eyes and mouth; mid-fifties = cheeks slightly sagging, deeper lines, a little grey at the temples.
Dress: in formal/official scenes a kamishimo of fine pale grey silk (stiff wide-shouldered sleeveless kataginu with matching hakama) over a white silk kimono, lighter and finer than the dark kamishimo of the lords around him; in private scenes a plain dark brown silk kimono and hakama. Always plain, without any crest, emblem or pattern. No armour. At most a closed folding fan.
