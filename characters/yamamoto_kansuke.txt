Yamamoto Kansuke: weathered, wiry Japanese strategist in his sixties, a career military
adviser rather than a front-line warrior. Deeply lined sun-darkened face, close-cropped
grey hair and short grey beard, thin hard mouth. He is blind in his right eye and wears a
simple dark cloth or leather eyepatch over it, tied plainly at the back of the head — a
clean, unambiguous silhouette rather than a closed or sunken eye (which reads poorly as a
small dark image and can look like an unintentional glitch). The patch itself is plain and
undecorated, no ornamentation. The left eye is sharp, alert and calculating. Depicted
quietly and without any grotesque or theatrical emphasis — no scarring spectacle, no
bandage wrapping the whole head, just the plain patch.
He stands with his weight carried on one side and leans on a plain wooden staff or the
shaft of a spear; the limp is suggested by posture only, never exaggerated or made
pitiable. Dignity and intelligence read first, the body second.
Dress: plain, unadorned Sengoku-period tosei gusoku armour in dull black or dark brown
lacquered plates with faded dark blue or grey cord lacing, worn over a coarse indigo
robe. NOT the red-lacquered Takeda akazonae armour. No ornate helmet crest, no gold,
no fur cloak — the deliberately austere look of an adviser, not a general.
Often shown with maps, terrain sketches, or a folding camp stool rather than a drawn sword.
Bearing: still, observant, entirely unimpressed by rank. A man who has spent his life
being underestimated and has stopped minding.
