Toyotomi Hideyoshi: short, slight male figure in his 40s-50s.
Distinctive narrow, fox-like face with alert, restless eyes that miss nothing.
Contemporaries compared him to a monkey — small, wiry, energetic.
Expression shifts rapidly from warmth and charm to cold calculation.
Wears magnificent golden armor in battle scenes; formal court robes in political scenes.
Carries himself with the confident authority of a man who earned every inch of power.
In early scenes (youth): rough peasant clothing, barefoot, watchful posture of a born observer.

Documented features (contemporary sources): Luis Frois described him as very short, plain-faced, with large protruding eyes and a sparse beard like a Chinese man; the Korean captive Kang Hang described him as small and monkey-like. Nobunaga jokingly called him a "bald rat" in a letter (c.1576), suggesting thinning hair even in middle age. Render: large, slightly protruding eyes; sparse thin moustache and small wispy goatee (never a full or thick beard); thin hair; shaved crown (sakayaki) with a small topknot. Paulownia (kiri) crest on robes/surcoat.

Late years (age 55-61, 1592-1598, e.g. the Hidetsugu incident, Daigo cherry-blossom party, deathbed): based on the 1598 Kano Mitsunobu portrait (Kodai-ji) and later portraits — gaunt and frail, hollow cheeks, deeply lined narrow face, large protruding eyes in hollow sockets, sparse grey-to-white moustache and thin wispy goatee, thin grey/white topknot. Mostly in dark formal court robes (no armor) in this period. Stooped, very small and thin; still sharp, watchful gaze.
Exception: in autumn 1591 (death of his son Tsurumatsu) he cut off his topknot in grief — in that scene only, no topknot, short loose hair.
