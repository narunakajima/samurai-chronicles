Otomo Sorin: tall, broad-shouldered Japanese daimyo. Physically confident and imposing in
early/mid-life scenes (20s-early 30s): strong build, dark hair in a standard Sengoku chonmage,
full black mustache and short beard, appraising and ambitious eyes. In later scenes (his final
decade, 50s): visibly aged — greying beard, deeper lines of exhaustion and doubt around the
eyes, a slight stoop, the same underlying strength now weighed down.

HAIR / TONSURE (important): in 1562, aged 33, he took Buddhist (Rinzai Zen) tonsure and the
religious name "Sorin" — the name this channel uses throughout. The only surviving contemporary
portrait (Zuihoin, Daitoku-ji, Kyoto) shows him with a fully SHAVEN head as a tonsured lay monk.
Therefore: scenes set before 1562 (his accession, the Xavier years, the early Portuguese trade)
= normal chonmage. Scenes set from 1562 onward — which is most of this story, including the
1570s peak, the 1578 baptism, Mimikawa, and his death — = completely shaven head, NOT a
chonmage. He keeps the mustache and beard throughout. He remained a shaven Zen lay monk even
after his Christian baptism; the shaven head and the cross appear together, and that visual
contradiction is the point.

Dress: standard Sengoku-period tosei gusoku lacquered armor in dark blue-black, bearing the
Otomo clan mon on the breastplate and helmet. NOT European cuirass or Western-style plate armor
— Sorin wears traditional Japanese battle dress even after his conversion. In court, diplomatic,
and domestic scenes: rich formal kosode and hakama in deep indigo or dark crimson silk, a wide
obi, a short ceremonial wakizashi at the hip.

Christian identity (from his baptism onward, roughly the episode's midpoint): wears a small,
plain silver or dark wooden cross on a cord, visible at the neckline of his robes or just above
his armor collar — subtle and personal, never a large or ornate crucifix, never combined with
any European clothing. No rosary unless a scene explicitly shows private prayer. Before his
baptism, no cross should appear.

Bearing: commanding, expansive, and confident in scenes of power; increasingly burdened and
inward-looking as the story turns toward Mimikawa and its aftermath — a man visibly carrying
the weight of a shrinking domain and an unresolved conscience.
